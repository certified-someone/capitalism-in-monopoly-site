What Is Capitalism? · The Rules of the Game

The Rules of the Game

A few players own properties. Everyone else pays to land on them. That’s capitalism, in miniature.

This certifies that private ownership entitles the holder to collect rent from all who land here.

Title Deed

Monopoly’s basic rule is simple: a few players own properties, everyone else pays to land on them. That’s more or less how capitalism works too. It’s a mode of production built on private ownership of factories, land, and equipment, which means most people end up selling their labor for a wage instead of owning a piece of what they make.

That’s not a one-time trade, it’s a loop. Something gets made, sold for more than it cost, and the profit gets reinvested to keep the cycle going which is what economists call capital accumulation. Monopoly runs the same loop: collect rent, build houses and hotels, collect more rent. Everyone starts with the same $1,500, but the board is designed so ownership snowballs once it gets moving.

The Rules

“Ownership snowballs once it gets moving.”

Three positions come out of that cycle. Capitalists accumulate profit. Laborers accumulate wages. Consumers accumulate goods. None of that comes down to talent or effort since it’s literally baked into the rules before anyone even sits down at the board. That’s why owning one full color set, which doubles rent on undeveloped property, puts you on a completely different track than someone still paying rent square by square.
